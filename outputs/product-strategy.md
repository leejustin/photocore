# Photocore product strategy

**One trip. Everyone's photos. One book.**

Written 2026-10-01. Photocore is the working name.

## The story in one paragraph

Every group trip ends up scattered across five camera rolls. Everyone has a slice. The best photo of you is on someone else's phone, the shared album turns into a dump nobody opens, and nobody ever makes the book. Collecting photos is solved: Apple, Google and a dozen wedding apps will gather them for free or close to it. Finishing them is not. Photocore finishes the trip. It picks your best photos on your own phone for free. Then it lets everyone who was there add theirs from a link, and turns the whole pile into one book: the best shot of every moment, repeats gone, each photo credited, and a short diary written only from what really happened.

## The problem

- **Photos are easy to take and hard to finish.** A weekend with friends easily produces several hundred photos across several phones. Choosing, removing repeats, putting them in order and writing a few words is hours of work, so it doesn't happen.
- **Group trips make it worse.** Each person holds part of the story. The photos of the photographer are on everyone else's phone. A shared album gathers them but leaves the mess for someone else to sort.
- **The tools stop at collection.** Apple Invites gives every event a shared album for up to 100 guests. Google Photos lets friends add to a Memory. Wedding apps charge $25 to $200 per event for a QR code that collects uploads. Of 12 wedding apps compared in one review, only one mentions AI organization and only one offers a printed book. Mixbook lets up to 30 people build a book together, entirely by hand.

## The insight

The value isn't in where the photos live. It's in the edit. Nobody wants a bigger album; they want the finished thing they'd never make themselves. A group makes that need sharper and makes the product spread on its own: every person who adds photos sees what Photocore made.

## The product: three acts

### Act 1. Your trip, on your phone, free

Photocore finds trips in your library and picks the best photos on the device. Nothing is uploaded or copied. A few swipes settle the close calls. You get a Photos album, snapshot cards and a highlight reel, all made with Apple's own tools on the phone.

This is the front door. It costs almost nothing to run, earns trust, and gives people a reason to open the app after every trip.

### Act 2. Bring everyone in

One tap makes an invite link. Friends open it in any browser, type a first name and add their photos. No app, no account, nothing to install. They can come back to add more or take theirs out. The owner sees who has added what and closes the link when the book is done.

Browser links matter. Vendor reports from the wedding market put guest participation at 65 to 85% with a browser link and 30 to 45% when an app is required.

### Act 3. The book

Photocore turns everyone's photos into one book:

- **The best shot of every moment.** Each person's photos are first culled against their own best, so a careful shooter and a burst shooter start even. When several people caught the same moment, only the best frame goes in.
- **Fair to everyone.** Friends share the space in turns, so one prolific friend can't take over.
- **Credited.** "Photos by Ana, Sam and Priya" on the cover and a name under each photo.
- **A diary that doesn't make things up.** It's written only from facts: places, times, landmarks, signs in the photos and what the owner said. People's names never go into it, so it can't credit the wrong person.
- **Two looks.** A clean book or Snapshots, printed photos pinned to a board.
- **Ready to share.** A private link, an Instagram set, a highlight reel, and prints so each person can order their own copy.

## Who it's for

**Start with friend-group trips.** These are weekends away, bachelor and bachelorette trips, reunions and family vacations. The buyer is the organizer: the person who booked the rental is the person who wants the book. Groups are 4 to 15 people, and everyone has a phone full of photos.

**Expand to:**

- **Family events** such as birthdays, holidays and graduations, where grandparents want prints.
- **Weddings** as a premium tier. It's a proven market that is used to paying per event, with more guests and higher stakes.
- **Teams and offsites** later, when there's demand.

**Not for:** professional photographers. They have Aftershoot and Lightroom, and the Mac studio stays as the owner's own pro tool.

## Business model

| Plan | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | On-device culling, albums, snapshot cards and the reel, always. Your first book, with up to 5 friends, in the Book style, with a "Made free with Photocore" footer. Online for a year. |
| Plus | $2.99 a month or $24.99 a year | Up to 12 books a year, 25 friends and 500 of their photos per book, both styles, the Instagram set, editing, no footer. Books stay up while you're subscribed and a year after. |
| Trip Pass | $4.99 once | One book with everything Plus gives it, kept for good |
| Event Pass | $29 once | One book for a wedding or big party, up to 100 guests, kept for good |
| Prints | Per order | Every friend can order their own copy. This is where the margin grows. |

Why this shape:

- **The free book is the growth loop.** Each one reaches every friend who added photos, so it isn't paywalled.
- **Plus is priced like things people already pay for.** $24.99 a year sits next to iCloud+ and VSCO Plus. It's offered at the moment someone finishes a book they love.
- **Passes catch people who hate subscriptions.** Photo apps keep only about 23% of annual subscribers (RevenueCat), because people use them in bursts. Trips are bursts.
- **Friends never pay.** Viewing a book and adding photos stay free. Only the organizer is ever asked.
- **Caps match real use and bound the worst case.** A book costs well under a dollar to make and host.
- **In-app purchase on iPhone.** The book is digital, so App Store rules require StoreKit. Physical prints can use Apple Pay with any print partner.

These prices are a starting point to test, not a decision.

## The growth loop

1. Someone culls a trip for free and likes it.
2. They invite friends to make the group book. Each friend sees Photocore at its best, with their own photos and their name in it.
3. The book page says "Make one for your trip." Friends who were impressed start their own next trip.
4. Prints spread it further: a physical book on a shelf, with friends asking where it came from.

**The one number:** new owners created per finished book. Above 1, the product grows on its own.

## What we promise

- **Your library stays yours.** Culling happens on your phone. Only what you choose to share is uploaded.
- **Nothing disappears in one go.** Set aside is an album you can undo. Deleting is capped and goes through iOS's own Recently Deleted.
- **Friends stay in control.** Anyone can remove the photos they added. Nobody needs an account.
- **No made-up stories.** The diary is built from facts, and anything it can't support is replaced.
- **Private by default, shared by choice.** Books are unlisted links, and invite links can be closed at any time.

This replaces "local only" as the headline. Local stays true for everything you do alone. The group book is hosted by nature, and we say so plainly.

## Why we win, and the honest risks

**Why we win:**

- **We finish; others collect.** Apple, Google and the QR apps own the pile. We own the result.
- **We work for everyone.** A browser link works for Android friends, and nobody needs an account.
- **The writing is hard to copy.** Grounded, careful writing and curation across several people's photos take real work to bolt onto a shared album.
- **It costs little.** On-device processing keeps the free tier nearly free, so we can afford to be generous.

**Risks:**

- **Apple or Google adds curation to their shared albums.** This is the biggest risk. Both already curate for their Memories features. Our answer is the finished book, prints and working across platforms. We should move fast and own "the group trip book" before they do.
- **People don't pay.** Collecting is free elsewhere. The validation test below has to show organizers will pay for a second book after their free one.
- **Consent and safety.** Photos of other people, and of children, need a clear policy, a way for anyone pictured to ask for removal, and moderation of uploads.
- **Storage costs.** Originals from many people add up. Upload windows, expiry and limits keep this in check.

## Roadmap

**Built today.** On-device cull, the streamed safety checks, set aside and restore, snapshot cards, the highlight reel, the hosted book with the grounded diary, two looks, invite links, curation across everyone, credits, and the live friends list in the app.

**Next 90 days: make it real and test it.**

1. Run 5 to 10 real group trips with friends of the team (see below).
2. Built: StoreKit 2 plans, server-checked purchases, owner links in the Keychain and a print-ready PDF. Next: App Store Server Notifications for refunds and renewals.
3. Background uploads, so leaving the app doesn't stop them.
4. An App Clip, so friends on iPhone run the same on-device cull before uploading, which makes their uploads smaller and gives them a taste of the free app.
5. A consent and removal policy, upload expiry, and basic moderation.
6. Move books off the test server to storage behind a CDN.

**After that: deepen and widen.**

- "Photos of me": each friend gets the photos they appear in.
- The Event Book tier for weddings.
- A print partner, with ordering for every contributor.
- A lock-screen progress view while the book is made, and "Make a book of my last trip" in Shortcuts and Siri.
- Android owners, by way of the web, if demand shows up.

## How we'll know it's working

Run it on 5 to 10 real group trips before building much more. Targets for continuing:

| Question | Target |
|---|---|
| Do invited friends actually upload? | 60% or more of them |
| Do books feel richer than one person's photos? | 2 or more contributors in most books, and the owner says it's better than the shared album |
| Would the organizer pay? | A meaningful share of organizers buy Plus or a Trip Pass after their free book |
| Does anyone want it on paper? | 15% or more of books get a print order or a clear request |
| Does it spread? | Some friends start their own trip within a month |

If friends don't upload, the group angle fails and the free cull becomes the product. If they upload but nobody pays, the book has to get better before the price is tested again.

## Open decisions

- **The name.** "Photocore" sounds like a pro tool. The product is warm and social. A new name should be checked for trademarks before use, as we learned with Polaroid.
- **The starting prices.** Test offering Plus first against offering the Trip Pass first.
- **How long friends' originals are kept.** This is a trade between cost and goodwill.
- **Where weddings fit.** They could stay a later premium tier, or become a second launch market if early demand pulls that way.
